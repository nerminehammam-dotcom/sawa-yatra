The four hardest cases

Star fields, sky gradients, dense street detail and altiplano texture. These are the four things JPEG is worst at. If they hold, everything else holds.

One thing I got wrong, and you should know it

When I wrote the scorecard I implied the image weights were hurting your visitors. I have now measured what a visitor actually downloads, and that was overstated.

Next.js re-encodes every photograph to AVIF or WebP at the width the browser asks for. At the sizes that dominate real traffic the difference between today's files and the new ones is almost nothing: 640px wide, 23 KB today against 22 KB proposed. 1024px wide, 75 KB against 75 KB. Only at the largest size, 1920px, does it matter — 347 KB against 302 KB, about 13 per cent.

So the honest case for doing this is not visitor speed. It is that your repository drops from 49 MB to about 30 MB, builds get faster, every file finally comes from a known master at a known setting instead of eleven different hand exports, and the largest renditions get meaningfully lighter. The real speed lever is elsewhere — making sure each photograph asks the browser for the width it is actually displayed at — and I will audit that separately.

What the pipeline will and will not do

  • Never writes to your master folders. They are read-only to this script, and they stay ignored by git, so they never leave your machine.
  • Never crops. Your masters run from 0.92 to 1.94 in aspect ratio — every one of those frames is a decision you already made. The script scales the long edge to 2000px and touches nothing else. No entropy cropping, no face detection, no straightening.
  • 2000px, and here is why that exact number: next.config.ts caps the widths it will ever request at 1920. So 2000 is the smallest source that never limits what a visitor can be served, and anything larger is weight nobody will ever see.
  • Re-runnable. It fingerprints each master, so re-running only regenerates what you have actually re-edited.
  • Dry run by default. It prints exactly what it would do and writes nothing until you add --write.
