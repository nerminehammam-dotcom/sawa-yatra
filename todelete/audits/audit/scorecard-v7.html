The shape of it

This is not a site that is uniformly half-finished. It is two sites. The craft is genuinely good — the colour system with its pairing table is better than anything I found on thirteen competitor sites, the typography is disciplined, 119 tests pass, the build is clean. The business layer barely exists: no price, no named founder, no legal copy, no working enquiry delivery, no analytics. A visitor cannot find out what it costs, who runs it, or what happens if they change their mind — and if they write to you anyway, the message goes nowhere.

That is a much better problem than the reverse. Craft is slow and expensive and you have it. The missing pieces are mostly decisions and paperwork, and three of them are things only you can do.

Five things earlier versions got wrong

Version 1 said two section cards showed a photograph from the wrong section. They did not. Those two files were not referenced by any page. The real cards come from the first photograph in each section's own gallery and were always correct. I should have checked the references before writing it down.

Version 1 implied the image weights were slowing your visitors down. Overstated. Measured afterwards: at 640px wide it is 23 KB before and 22 KB after; at 1024px it is identical. Only the 1920px rendition improves, by about 13%. The honest case for that work is repository weight, build time and consistency.

The comment at the top of tokens.css was itself slightly wrong, and it is your file rather than mine. It claimed ink is the only colour that pairs safely with the rest. Ink is safe against five of seven — ink on signal-text is 2.57, ink on clay is 3.75 — and paper takes signal-text as well as ink, which the prose never said. Corrected in the file.

Version 1 said focus styles appeared in only 11 of 43 stylesheets. Misleading. A global focus rule has always existed; component files simply do not repeat it. The three outline: none rules are all on :focus, on headings focused in code — correct practice, not a defect. The real gaps were narrower.

Version 1 said nothing but Travel Self had a share card. Wrong again. Every page had one — it was the same one on all thirty-six, which is a worse problem and a more interesting one.

What has not changed: the four launch blockers. Legal copy, the money sentence, form delivery, and who runs this. Every one needs a decision or a signature from you, which is why four days of my work has not touched any of them.

What 53 other sites taught me

27 travel operators, 13 members' clubs, 13 photography and gallery sites, all visited on 6 August 2026. The item-by-item lessons are inside the cards above. These are the five findings that cut across all of them.

1  ·  Nobody is good at both pictures and facts. That gap is yours.

The brands with the best photography publish the fewest facts. Belmond's Andean Explorer has picture-desk-grade imagery and alt text that describes depth of field — and no price, no dates, no group size, no map, no altitude, on a 4,000 m route. Chimu publish a difficulty rating, meal counts and live availability — and ship raw unresized JPEGs with spaces in the filenames. In 27 sites there was no example of a brand doing both. You have the photographs and you already have the altitude data. That combination is unoccupied.

2  ·  Exclusivity and price transparency are not in conflict.

Exclusive Resorts publish a $195,000 initiation fee on a page called How It Works — and admit fewer than 200 families a year. Kindred put "Pricing" in the primary nav, state there is no membership fee, prove it with four worked examples, and still require verification and application. The scarcity lives in who is admitted, not in what it costs to ask. Eleven of thirteen clubs publish some kind of number; the two that publish nothing are agencies selling a relationship, not a defined route.

3  ·  No one promises a response time. Everyone offers a human.

Across all thirteen clubs the strongest commitment is "shortly" — no SLA, no 48 hours, nothing. But every single one has a phone number, and most have a second lower-commitment door: WhatsApp at Cookson, "call me back" at Untold Story, "schedule a call" at Pelorus. The absence of a response-time promise is always paid for with the presence of a person. You currently have neither, which is the real gap — not the missing SLA.

4  ·  Scarcity is a number, never an adjective.

200 new members a year. 30–40 projects. 4,000 members. 11 rooms. Three guests per two guides. 359 shoots in 133 countries. 300,000 exclusive acres. Nobody writes "highly exclusive" — they write a count and let the reader do the arithmetic. Your model is a scarcity claim with no number attached to it yet. You already have candidates: 71 days, 9 sections, 3 countries, 12 travellers, 4,910 m.

5  ·  Serious photography sites never pin both dimensions of an image.

Fraenkel Gallery fixes height at 900px and lets width run 619 to 1170 — the digital version of hanging prints on a common centre line. Panos fixes width and lets height float from 221 to 707. Of thirteen photography and gallery sites, not one pins both, not one rounds a corner, and not one draws anything on top of a photograph. Your own site already does the elegant version of the caption: title under the plate, medium and date once per series, full credit in the alt. The travel site should be copying it more faithfully than it currently is.

Everything that is left

Eleven aspects are closed. Every remaining one needs a decision, a signature or a phone — none of them needs another day of my analysis.

  1. Commission the legal copy

    Longest lead time of anything here, and it gates the forms. Privacy, terms, cookie basis. A solicitor — not me, not an AI. Their brief is already written in docs/TURN-THE-FORMS-ON.md.

  2. Decide the money sentence

    Not a price list. One sentence. And when you have it, price the complete Caravan below the sum of its sections and say so on the page — Dragoman's whole route is £1,195 cheaper than its halves and they never mention it.

  3. Turn the forms on

    Resend account, three variables in Vercel, redeploy. Thirty minutes. Until then every enquiry fails, visibly.

  4. Put your name on it

    The photographs are now credited to the route. They are not yet credited to you.

  5. Grade the nine sections

    The largest unoccupied position in your exact geography: nobody in the Andes publishes a difficulty grade. Give me a scale and nine numbers and I will build the rest. I will not invent a physical grading for a 4,910 m pass.

  6. Cut the pages you are not launching

    Twelve pages are under 14 lines. Tell me which are not launching and they go, with their nav entries. An hour, and the site stops looking half-built.

  7. Push, then ten minutes on your phone

    A day of commits exists in exactly one place. Then open the preview on your phone, and run axe DevTools on the deployed site.
